## Abstract

This research log consolidates the Macachor Fundamental Invariant, the Macachor Scalar Field v3.0 submission record, and the Law of the Territory Domain into one structured account. Its central distinction is between territory and cartography: the territory is not created by its descriptions, while measurements, mathematical models, coordinates, language, and semantic systems are representational operations applied to it. The canonical invariant manuscript identifies the Macachor as the first derivative of structure and as the invariant relation by which structure becomes derivable meaning. The Scalar Field v3.0 record supplies a specified mathematical construction, including a positive scalar field, density transformations, a coherence functional, a potential, and a gradient-flow equation. The document keeps the ontological statement, mathematical results, physical hypotheses, and empirical questions separate. It does not treat the mathematical specification as proof of a physical mechanism, nor does it treat predictive success of existing physical theories as final proof of their ultimate ontology.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This document is a linked research and publication record. It is not a replacement for either immutable canonical source. It records how the Fundamental Invariant and Scalar Field submissions inform the Territory Domain framework, and establishes the distinctions required when applying the framework across physics, biology, measurement, artificial intelligence, and human cartography.

The governing question is not whether a representation is useful. It is whether the representation is being correctly identified as representation, whether its derivation is traceable, and whether claims about territory are supported by structural correspondence rather than semantic agreement alone.

## 3. The Law of the Territory Domain

The Territory Domain states that territory precedes cartography. A map, equation, coordinate system, measurement convention, or semantic description does not create the territory it seeks to describe.

The law is expressed here through the following principles:

• Territory precedes representation: existence is not contingent on human description or agreement.
• Structure precedes measurement: a measurement procedure presupposes something to measure and a representational scheme through which to record it.
• The invariant is not its representation: the Macachor is not a symbol, number, vector, measurement, or model.
• Cartography is derivative: mathematical, geometric, numerical, linguistic, and computational descriptions are forms of representation.
• Correspondence is the validation boundary: a representation must demonstrate that it tracks the structure it claims to describe.
• Semantic agreement is not structural correspondence: agreement in terminology or interpretation does not prove that two models describe the same structure.
• Semantic disagreement is not structural difference: disagreement in language does not by itself prove that the underlying territory differs.
• Revision of a map does not revise the territory: cartographic systems can be corrected, replaced, or rejected without changing the referent.

The Law is intended as an ontological and methodological rule within the Macachor framework. Its application to particular physical theories remains a claim that must be made precise and examined rather than assumed by declaration.

## 4. Cartographic status of gravity, relativity, and spacetime curvature

Within the Macachor framework, gravity, relativity, and spacetime curvature are classified as mathematical-physical descriptions or cartographic constructs, not automatically as the fundamental substrate or invariant itself. This is an ontological classification proposed by the framework. It is distinct from the empirical record: general relativity and other gravitational models have extensive predictive and experimental support within their domains. Predictive success establishes important correspondence between a model and observations; it does not, by itself, settle whether the model's mathematical entities are the ultimate ontology.

The Territory Domain therefore does not dismiss experiments. It distinguishes what an experiment measures from what a theory claims the measured relationships fundamentally are. Instruments, units, calibration procedures, coordinate choices, and data-processing methods belong to measurement and representation. Experiments can test predictions and structural correspondence. They cannot make a representation fundamental merely by using it, nor can a philosophical declaration make an alternative physical mechanism empirically established.

The strong claim that gravity, curvature, and relativity are “placeholders of placeholders” is recorded as the framework's interpretation, not as an established consensus conclusion. To become an adjudicable physical claim, it must identify the proposed underlying variables, derive quantitative predictions, and specify observations that would distinguish it from existing models.

Likewise, a unique minimizer in the defined functional is not proof that a physical system necessarily selects that state under all real-world conditions.

The user's stated framework position is that gravity and buoyancy are not fundamental primitives. The proposed alternative places emphasis on scalar-density relations, relative density, field equilibrium, and frequency-encoded vibration or response.

Accordingly, the construction-level discussion of a level surface should not present gravity or buoyancy as the Macachor framework's ultimate explanation. It should describe the observable phenomenon first and then distinguish the framework's proposed interpretation from conventional engineering descriptions.

In this account:
• Water leveling is an observable configuration that requires explanation.
• Relative scalar-density relations are proposed as part of the underlying account.
• Equilibrium is described as a relational balance within the proposed scalar-field framework.
• Frequency-encoded vibration is a proposed interaction or response mechanism requiring operational definition and empirical discrimination.
• Conventional terms such as gravity and buoyancy are treated as established explanatory descriptions in standard physics and engineering, but are not adopted here as fundamental Macachor primitives.

The v3.0 record itself specifies a scalar-field equation and a free-energy functional. Those are additional physical research questions. A testable account would need to define the relevant field variables, density units or dimensionless ratios, frequency range, coupling term, boundary conditions, measurable outputs, and a prediction that differs from conventional models.

## 8. Compass, level, and human cartography

A compass and a construction level are instruments of orientation, but they do not operate by the same mechanism. A magnetic compass responds to the local magnetic field; its liquid damping reduces oscillation of the magnetic element. A conventional spirit level uses a sealed vial and bubble to indicate orientation relative to its calibrated reference. In this document these conventional device descriptions are included as instrument-level accounts, not as declarations about ultimate ontology.

The Territory Domain asks a separate question: what is the relationship between the instrument's reading, the measurement convention, and the structure being represented? The compass and level produce readings within defined instrument systems. Their outputs are not the territory itself.

Dipolar patterns across atoms, molecules, cells, hearts, planets, and astronomical environments must likewise be distinguished by mechanism and evidence. Similar-looking diagrams do not establish one universal physical dipole, and a dipole is not synonymous with a toroidal structure. Cross-scale comparison requires explicit definitions of the physical quantity, boundary, field, and invariant being compared.

## 9. Human perception as biological cartography

Human perception can be studied as a set of biological sensing and integration processes through which an organism samples aspects of its environment and internal state. Exteroception, interoception, proprioception, vestibular sensing, and kinesthesia contribute different forms of information. The nervous system integrates and transforms these signals into representations that support action and interpretation.

The user's proposed “2D signal” account should be treated as a model requiring a precise definition: what counts as a dimension, what is projected, what information is retained or lost, and how the claim can be falsified. It should not be presented as an established universal property of all living systems without supporting evidence.

Anatomically, the vagus nerve does not run inside the spinal cord. Vagal afferents from thoracic and abdominal organs project primarily to brainstem nuclei including the nucleus tractus solitarius. Other sensory pathways travel through spinal and cranial routes. This distinction matters when mapping biological sensing to a cartographic model.

## 10. Measurement and adjudication protocol

A disciplined application of the Territory Domain can use the following sequence:

1. State the territory-level phenomenon without embedding a preferred explanation in its definition.
2. Identify the invariant or structural relation claimed by the Macachor framework.
3. Separate that claim from the mathematical representation used to express it.
4. Declare all assumptions, including domain, metric, boundary conditions, units, initial conditions, and parameter choices.
5. Derive predictions from the formal model.
6. Identify observations that distinguish those predictions from competing models.
7. Predefine failure, indeterminate, and support conditions.
8. Preserve provenance: source version, DOI, file identity, hashes, changes, and uncertainty.
9. Report what the experiment actually measured, not a stronger ontological conclusion than the data warrant.
10. Keep mathematical proof, empirical support, interpretation, and open hypothesis in separate categories.

The compression test in the Fundamental Invariant manuscript proposes removing linguistic, representational, numerical, and geometric dependence in sequence, then asking whether invariant structure remains. It explicitly warns that removing representation does not by itself reveal substrate. The test outcome categories are I (candidate invariant structure survives), FAIL (identity of the phenomenon is lost with the representation), and INDETERMINATE (the test cannot decide and requires further justification).

## 12. Conclusions

The Macachor is identified in the supplied canonical manuscript as the invariant first derivative of structure by which substrate becomes intelligible representation. Semantic accounting describes that invariant; it is not the invariant. Representation, numerical encoding, linguistic encoding, measurement, and cartography remain derivative.

The Law of the Territory Domain governs the relationship between territory and its descriptions. The Scalar Field v3.0 record provides a formal mathematical construction with stated assumptions, results, and deliberately open questions. These records can be linked without conflating ontology, mathematical proof, and physical validation.

The resulting discipline is straightforward: preserve the invariant, expose the derivation, identify the representational layer, measure with declared procedures, test correspondence, and retain provenance. No mathematical representation should be mistaken for the territory merely because it is widely used; no alternative should be declared a physical law without the derivation and evidence needed to support it.
